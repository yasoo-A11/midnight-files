You were told
Black Friday is the day
stores go into the black.
That’s a cover story.
The first Black Friday
was a disaster.
1869.
Two financiers try to
corner America’s gold,
and the market collapses.
Almost a century later,
the name returns
in Philadelphia.
The day after Thanksgiving,
the suburbs pour
into the city.
Shoppers,
football fans,
gridlock.
Police can’t take
the day off.
Long shifts,
traffic,
shoplifters.
So they give the day
a name:
Black Friday.
It’s not a compliment.
The stores hate it.
In 1961,
they try a new name:
Big Friday.
Nobody listens.
So in the ’80s,
retailers keep the name
and rewrite the story.
Red ink to black ink.
Profit.
Last year,
200 million Americans
shopped that weekend
under a name
coined by cops
who hated it.
What else were you sold
that was never true?
