# Sources: the turkey pardon is younger than you think

| Claim | Sources |
|---|---|
| Tad Lincoln asked his father to spare a turkey meant for a holiday meal; the bird was a Christmas turkey (1863), and it is not the origin of the yearly pardon | History.com, "A Brief History of the Presidential Turkey Pardon"; White House Historical Association; Snopes |
| From 1947 the National Turkey Federation (poultry industry) has presented a turkey to the president each year, originally for eating | History.com; White House Historical Association; NPR (2019) |
| Truman is often credited with the first pardon, but the Truman Library found no evidence he pardoned a turkey | NPR (2019, 2024); Snopes; History.com |
| November 19, 1963: the turkey wore a sign reading "Good eating, Mr. President"; Kennedy said "We'll just let this one grow"; newspapers called it a pardon | History.com; White House Historical Association; National Constitution Center |
| November 17, 1989: George H. W. Bush formally pardoned the turkey, the first official pardon; it has been yearly since | History.com; NPR; Axios; National Constitution Center |
| "37 years old" on screen: 1989 to 2026 (the narration says "under forty") | arithmetic from the 1989 date above |
