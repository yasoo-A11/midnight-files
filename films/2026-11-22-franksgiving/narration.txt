Thanksgiving is on the fourth Thursday of November. That rule came out of a fight about shopping. In 1863, Lincoln picks the last Thursday. For seventy years, nobody touches it. Then, 1939. November has five Thursdays, so Thanksgiving lands on the thirtieth. The Great Depression isn't over, and stores want more days to sell before Christmas. So the president moves Thanksgiving a week earlier: November twenty-third. The country splits. About half the states follow him. The rest keep the old date. Critics call it Franksgiving. Texas, Colorado and Mississippi can't decide, so they celebrate twice. Two years later, the government checks the numbers: no real boost in sales. So in 1941, Congress locks it to the fourth Thursday. It's still there. This year, that's November twenty-sixth. Would you move a holiday to sell more?
