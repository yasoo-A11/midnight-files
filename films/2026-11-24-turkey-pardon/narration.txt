Every November, an American president pardons a turkey. People say it goes back to Lincoln. It doesn't. Lincoln's son did beg him to spare a turkey once, but it was a Christmas bird. In nineteen forty-seven, the turkey industry starts sending the president a bird every year. Not a pet. Dinner. Many people credit Truman with the first pardon. His own presidential library found no record of it. Then, nineteen sixty-three. A turkey arrives with a sign around its neck: Good eating, Mr. President. Kennedy looks at it and says: we'll just let this one grow. Newspapers call it a pardon. It isn't official. That only comes in nineteen eighty-nine, when the first President Bush promises the bird will not end up on anyone's table. Since then, it happens every year. A tradition that feels ancient is under forty years old. What else feels older than it really is?
