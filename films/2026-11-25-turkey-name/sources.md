# Sources: why the turkey is called a turkey

| Claim | Sources |
|---|---|
| The turkey is native to North America (domesticated in Mexico); Spanish ships brought it to Europe in the early 1500s | National Geographic, "Fowl play: the twisted linguistics of turkey" (2015); Scientific American, "Talkin' Turkey"; The Week |
| The African guinea fowl reached Europe earlier through Ottoman/Turkish trade and was called "turkey cock" / "turkey hen"; the American bird was taken for the same kind of bird and inherited the name | National Geographic; Dictionary.com, "Which Turkey came first"; The Week; History Facts (2025); Omniglot |
| Turkish: hindi ("from India") | National Geographic; The Week; VOA Learning English; Dictionary.com; Anadolu Agency |
| French: dinde, from (poule/coq) d'Inde, "of India" | National Geographic; The Week; Scientific American; Dictionary.com |
| Portuguese: peru | Scientific American; VOA Learning English; Dictionary.com; Anadolu Agency |
| Arabic: dik rumi, "Roman rooster/chicken" | The Week; Daily Sabah / Anadolu Agency; uTalk |
| "4 wrong places" on screen: Turkey, India, Peru, Rome, from the four names above; the bird is native to none of them | from the rows above |
