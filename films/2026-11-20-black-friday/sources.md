# Sources: Why it is called Black Friday

Checked on 2026-11-20.

| Claim in the film | Source |
| --- | --- |
| The first "Black Friday" was the gold panic of 24 September 1869, set off by Jay Gould and Jim Fisk trying to corner the gold market | Britannica, https://www.britannica.com/event/Black-Friday ; History.com, https://www.history.com/articles/black-friday-thanksgiving-origins-history |
| Gold fell from about $160-$163 to $133 within minutes once the Treasury sold | History.com, https://www.history.com/articles/the-black-friday-gold-scandal-145-years-ago ; Britannica (peak $163.50) |
| Philadelphia police used the name in the 1950s for the day after Thanksgiving: suburban shoppers and visitors arriving before the Army-Navy game, traffic, no day off, extra-long shifts, shoplifters | History.com (same article) ; Philadelphia Inquirer, https://www.inquirer.com/archive/black-friday-thanksgiving-origins-history-philly-20221122.html |
| Twelve-hour shifts | WGBH, https://www.wgbh.org/news/local/2015-11-25/how-black-friday-got-its-name-its-not-what-you-think ; NBC Philadelphia, https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/news/local/why-black-fridays-called-black-friday/2079172/ |
| In 1961 merchants tried to rename it "Big Friday" and failed | History.com ; CNN Money, https://money.cnn.com/2014/11/28/news/black-friday-history/ |
| The "red ink to black ink" explanation appeared in the late 1980s and is not the origin | History.com ; NBC Philadelphia |
| A record 202.9 million people shopped over the five-day Thanksgiving weekend in 2025 | National Retail Federation, https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/thanksgiving-holiday-weekend-draws-a-record-203-million-shoppers |

Dating note: History.com and CNN (citing researcher Bonnie Taylor-Blake) say the 1950s; the Inquirer says the late 1950s or
early 1960s; NBC Philadelphia says the early 1960s. The film says "the 1950s" and shows no single year.
