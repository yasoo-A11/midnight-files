You were told Black Friday is the day stores go into the black. That's a cover story. The first Black Friday was a disaster: eighteen sixty-nine. Two financiers try to corner America's gold, and the market collapses. Almost a century later, the name returns, in Philadelphia. The day after Thanksgiving, the suburbs pour into the city: shoppers, football fans, gridlock. Police can't take the day off. Long shifts, traffic, shoplifters. So they give the day a name: Black Friday. It's not a compliment. The stores hate it. In nineteen sixty-one, they try a new name: Big Friday. Nobody listens. So in the eighties, retailers keep the name and rewrite the story: red ink to black ink. Profit. Last year, two hundred million Americans shopped that weekend, under a name coined by cops who hated it. What else were you sold that was never true?
