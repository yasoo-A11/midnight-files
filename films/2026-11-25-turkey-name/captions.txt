Tomorrow, millions
of Americans eat a turkey.
The bird has nothing
to do with Turkey.
It comes from
North America.
So why the name?
Blame a different bird.
In the 1500s,
Europe already knew
an African bird,
the guinea fowl.
It came in
through Ottoman trade,
so the English called it
the turkey cock.
Then Spanish ships
bring home a bigger bird
from the New World.
It looks similar.
People assume
it is the same thing,
and the name sticks.
And the Turks?
They knew it wasn't theirs,
so they call it hindi,
the bird from India.
The French say dinde,
from India, again.
In Portuguese, it's peru.
In Arabic, dik rumi,
the Roman rooster.
One bird from America,
named after four places
it never came from.
What would you
have called it?
