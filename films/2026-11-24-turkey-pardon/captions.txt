Every November,
an American president
pardons a turkey.
People say it goes
back to Lincoln.
It doesn't.
Lincoln's son did beg him
to spare a turkey once,
but it was
a Christmas bird.
In 1947,
the turkey industry
starts sending the president
a bird every year.
Not a pet, dinner.
Many people credit Truman
with the first pardon.
His own
presidential library
found no record of it.
Then, 1963,
a turkey arrives
with a sign around its neck.
Good eating,
Mr. President.
Kennedy looks at it
and says,
we'll just
let this one grow.
Newspapers call it
a pardon.
It isn't official.
That only comes
in 1989,
when the first
President Bush promises
the bird will not end up
on anyone's table.
Since then,
it happens every year.
A tradition
that feels ancient
is under 40 years old.
What else feels older
than it really is?
