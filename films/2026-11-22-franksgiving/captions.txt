Thanksgiving is on
the fourth Thursday
of November.
That rule came out
of a fight
about shopping.
In 1863,
Lincoln picks
the last Thursday.
For 70 years,
nobody touches it.
Then, 1939.
November has
five Thursdays,
so Thanksgiving lands
on the 30th.
The Great Depression
isn't over,
and stores want
more days to sell
before Christmas.
So the president
moves Thanksgiving
a week earlier,
November 23rd.
The country splits.
About half the states
follow him,
the rest keep
the old date.
Critics call it
Franksgiving.
Texas, Colorado,
and Mississippi
can't decide,
so they celebrate twice.
Two years later,
the government
checks the numbers.
No real boost
in sales.
So in 1941,
Congress locks it
to the fourth Thursday.
It's still there.
This year, that's
November 26th.
Would you move
a holiday
to sell more?
